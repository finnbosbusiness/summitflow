01 — Mindset Foundation

Fear is an illusion. Push through it.

You're cold calling heat pump business owners all day. They'll say no. They'll be abrupt. Some will hang up. None of that means anything. Volume is the only variable you control.

"It is completely normal to be riddled with fear when it comes to cold calling. Human beings really don't like being told no. But this is precisely why cold calls are exceptional for new entrepreneurs — they will build the resilience you need to be successful long term." — Jordan Platten

Acknowledge the Fear

It's okay to be afraid. Accept it as part of the process. Then dial anyway. The call is neutral — it's the story you tell yourself that makes it feel dangerous.

It's a Numbers Game

At 4–5% meeting rate, you need 20–25 conversations for one meeting. You get 100 dials in, you're statistically getting 4–5 conversations with decision makers. That's 1 meeting per day at minimum.

They Need You

MCS installers want more survey appointments. You're not interrupting them — you're offering them a system that fills their calendar. Approach it with that conviction.

⚡

The truth: You won't break imposter syndrome until you sign your first clients and deliver results. Imposter syndrome is a sign of growth. Keep going. The first client is on the other side of this.

02 — Environment Setup

Create the perfect calling environment

None of the tactics matter if your environment handicaps you. Body follows breath. Voice follows body. Get this right first.

🎧

Use Handsfree

AirPods, earphones, or headphones. Holding a phone closes you off. Handsfree = freedom of expression = conviction in your voice.

🧍

Stand Up & Pace

Never sit hunched. Standing opens your diaphragm, improves airflow, creates a stronger voice. Your energy is completely different standing.

🤫

Eliminate Distractions

Quiet room. No other tabs open. No music. No one else in the room who might judge you. Perceived judgment kills conviction.

😊

Smile While You Dial

They can't see it but they can hear it. Smiling adds warmth and upward warmth to your voice. It changes the energy of the call.

Pre-Call Checklist

  • Quiet room — door closed, no distractions
  • Handsfree connected and working
  • Standing position, space to pace
  • Only MCS lead list open on screen
  • Script nearby but not staring at it
  • Deep breath in, long breath out, "I've got this"
  • Smile on your face before the first dial
💡

Habit Stack: Attach cold calling to your morning routine. Coffee down → stand up → first batch of dials. Automatic within 2 weeks. 8am sharp.

03 — The Two Characters

You play two roles on every call

You cannot speak to a receptionist the same way you speak to the business owner. Your tone shifts. Your character shifts.

The Old Friend
For Gatekeepers

You're calling like you already know the owner. Confident, casual, warm. You don't explain. You assume familiarity.

Mindset: "Imagine calling a mate's business and the admin picks up. How would you speak to them?"

CasualFamiliarBriefDirective
The Trusted Expert
For Decision Makers

You have inside knowledge and genuinely want to help them grow. Calm authority. Clear. Composed. No fluff.

Mindset: "You are equal to them — not below, not above. You have something they actually need."

AuthoritativeComposedConvictedPeer-Level

❌ Never Say to Gatekeepers

  • "Hi, can I speak to the owner please?"
  • Your company name unless asked
  • Anything about marketing or lead generation
  • Any full explanation of why you're calling

✅ What Actually Works

  • "Hey, is [Name] about?"
  • "[Name] will know what it's regarding"
  • "It's a bit complex to explain right now, could you pop me through?"
  • Be beautifully vague. Never reveal your cards.
04 — Tonality Mastery

70% of your first impression is tone

It's not the words you say — it's how you say them. Your voice is the lens through which they judge your intentions, energy, and worth.

The Five Voice Controls

1. Pitch — Communicates Emotion

Higher
Lower

Higher pitch = excitement, urgency. Lower pitch = confidence, calm, control. Use lower for statements of certainty.

2. Pace — Shows Control

Fast
Slow

Fast = nervous, trying to get through before they hang up. Slow = confident, comfortable. Slow down. Always.

3. Inflection — Controls Certainty

Downward inflection = conviction, certainty. Upward inflection = doubt, seeking approval. End your statements like you mean them.

"It's Finn from SummitFlow↓" — Authority, confidence "It's Finn from SummitFlow↑?" — Uncertain, seeking validation

⚡ SummitFlow Call Script — MCS Heat Pump Installers

Stage 1: Gatekeeper (Old Friend Character)
"Hey, is [Owner Name] about?" Casual, downward inflection. Like calling a friend. Never say "please" or "the owner." [If pressed: "What's this regarding?"] "Just tell them it's Finn calling — they'll know what it's about." Confident, familiar. Never reveal you're selling. [If pushed harder:] "It's a bit complex to explain right now, could you pop me through?"
Stage 2: Validate Decision Maker
"Am I speaking to [Name]?" Confirm you're with the right person before proceeding. Don't waste your pitch on a junior.
Stage 3: Diffuse
"[Name], I know you're busy so I'll be really quick." Downward inflection. Shows respect. Positions you as someone who values their time.
Stage 4: Attention Hook — Choose One
Option A — Competitor Hook (Highest Converting) "Have you seen what some of the bigger installation companies are doing to guarantee their survey pipeline throughout the year?" Triggers competitive instinct. Installers hate competitors doing better. Pause and let them respond.
Option B — Capacity Hook "Could you handle an extra 8–10 qualified heat pump surveys booked into your calendar next month?" "Could you handle" feeds into ego — of course they can. Opens desire-based conversation. Pause for response.
Option C — BUS Grant Hook "Are you making the most of the Boiler Upgrade Scheme demand? There are homeowners in your area qualifying for the £7,500 grant right now and actively searching for MCS installers." Creates immediate relevance. BUS grants mean motivated homeowners — not cold leads.
Stage 5: Elevator Pitch
"Perfect. So I help MCS-certified heat pump installers across the UK get guaranteed qualified survey appointments booked directly into their calendar." [PAUSE — let that land] "We run targeted outreach to homeowners actively researching heat pump installations in your area — people who already qualify for the BUS grant..." [PAUSE] "...then our AI qualifies them and books them straight into your survey calendar." [PAUSE] "The whole thing's automated — so you get a consistent flow of qualified surveys without spending time chasing leads." Slow. Micro-pauses between key points. Downward inflection on certainties. Don't rush through this.
Stage 6: Book the Meeting
"I've got some ideas I'd love to share with you — you can take them and implement yourself if you like." "Are you free for 15 minutes tomorrow at [specific time]?" Lead with value. Specific time only — never "sometime this week." [If they agree:] "Perfect. What's the best email to send the invite to? And your mobile — just in case I need to reach you?" Always get both email AND mobile. Send the invite while still on the call.
Stage 7: Risk Reversal (Use for Hesitation)
"And just so you know — we guarantee a minimum number of qualified surveys per month on retainer. If we don't deliver, you don't pay." "What's a completed installation worth to you? £8,000? £12,000?" [Let them answer] "So if we're delivering 8 qualified surveys a month, and you convert even 30% of those — that's 2–3 installations worth £15–30k. For a retainer that costs a fraction of that. Does that maths make sense?" Performance-backed retainer removes risk. The ROI argument is compelling — a single install covers months of retainer.
🔑

The Golden Rule: Never sell on a cold call. Never discuss pricing. Never pitch the full service. Your only goal is to book a 15-minute meeting. Everything else happens on the Zoom.

❌ Never Say These

  • "This is a cold call, do you want to hang up or give me 30 seconds?" — Terrible positioning
  • "I'm not trying to sell you anything" — Weak, self-degrading
  • "If you're interested..." — No conviction
  • "I'm not sure if this is relevant for you" — Why are you calling then?
  • "If you're free, maybe we could..." — Wishy-washy

✅ Say These Instead

  • "I'm confident we can get you more qualified surveys" — Conviction
  • "What have you got to lose?" — Challenges their logic
  • "Are you free tomorrow at 2pm?" — Specific, assumptive
  • "Worst case, you take these ideas and implement yourself" — No risk frame
  • "I know we can make this work for you" — Certainty
06 — Objection Handling

The sale starts at the first objection

Don't fight objections — redirect them. Agree with their concern, then offer a different perspective. Push back 2–3 times before accepting a no. Most give up at one.

"Not interested" / "We're fine for leads"

The auto-reject. Don't accept it. They haven't heard the value yet.

"I completely get that. Can I ask — are you fully booked every single month, or are there quieter periods when you could do with more surveys in the calendar?"

[Let them answer — most will admit to quieter periods]

"That's exactly what we solve. We give you a consistent baseline so you're never dependent on word-of-mouth or referrals. What have you got to lose by having a 15-minute chat?"

"We already have a marketing company"

Acknowledge, then differentiate hard.

"That's great — we're not doing general marketing. We specifically guarantee qualified heat pump survey appointments delivered directly into your calendar. Very different from a marketing agency running ads and hoping for the best."

"What's your current agency delivering in terms of guaranteed surveys per month?"

If they can't answer, you've made the distinction. If they can, acknowledge it and find the gap.
"We're busy enough / don't need more work"

Probe the assumption.

"That's brilliant — are you booked solid 12 months of the year, or are there seasonal dips?"

[They'll almost always admit to seasonal slowdown]

"The quieter months are exactly when a consistent survey pipeline matters most. We help you smooth out those dips so you've always got work in the calendar. Worst case, you have too many surveys and pick the best ones."

"We tried ads before and it didn't work"

Common and valid. Acknowledge, then differentiate your model.

"I hear that a lot — most agencies run generic ads that attract time-wasters. Can I ask what happened with yours?"

[Let them vent]

"The difference with what we do is we specifically target homeowners who already qualify for the BUS grant and are actively searching for MCS installers. Not cold traffic. Pre-qualified, motivated homeowners. And we only charge when the surveys land in your calendar."

The BUS grant angle is your differentiator. High-intent leads who already have the grant motivation are very different from Facebook ad leads.
"We get leads from [manufacturer / Vaillant / Worcester Bosch]"

Position as complementary, not replacement.

"That's brilliant — manufacturer leads are great. But can I ask, are those enough to keep your engineers busy 12 months of the year? Or do you find they're inconsistent?"

[They almost always admit inconsistency]

"We don't replace your manufacturer relationship at all — we give you a predictable baseline on top of it. So you're never solely dependent on one source. Think of it as your survey pipeline insurance."

"Haven't got time right now"

They're trying to end the call. Get a specific future time.

"No problem — when would be a better time to give you a ring back?"

[Get a specific day and time]

Or push once: "Look, just before you go — I literally only need 20 seconds. There are homeowners in your area qualifying for the BUS grant right now who can't find an MCS installer. You're going to want to hear this."

Never leave without either a meeting booked or a specific callback time. Always get their mobile.
"Need to think about it" / Vague brush-off

"Absolutely, I get that. Can I ask — what specifically would you want to think about? Is it whether the BUS grant demand is real in your area, or how the survey qualification works, or something else?"

[Now you can address the real concern]

"[Name], there's literally no risk — we guarantee surveys on retainer. Every day you wait is another day of homeowners in your area signing with another installer. What would need to happen for this to be a no-brainer?"

💪

The Push-Back Rule: Push back 2–3 times before accepting rejection. Most salespeople give up after one "no." The best approach calls with the energy that the prospect needs them more than they need the prospect — without arrogance.

07 — Securing The Meeting

Never leave without a next step

Your only goal on every call is a meeting. Nothing else matters.

Meeting Framework

  1. 1 Lead with value: "I've got some ideas I'd love to share — you can take them and implement yourself."
  2. 2 Specific time: Never "sometime this week." Always "tomorrow at 2pm" or "Friday at 10am."
  3. 3 As soon as possible: Every extra day = lower show rate. Book within 48 hours where possible.
  4. 4 Get email AND mobile: Both. Send the calendar invite while still on the call.
Booking Line "I've got some ideas I'd love to share — you can take them and implement yourself if you like. Are you free for 15 minutes tomorrow at 10am?" Get Details "Perfect. What's the best email to send the invite? And your mobile — just in case I need to reach you?" Confirm "Great — I'm sending that now. Are you at your desk? Can you check you've received it?"
